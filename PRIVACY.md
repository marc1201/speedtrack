# Privacy policy

Speedtrack is an iPhone app published by Eins-zwei-null-eins Portugal Unipessoal, Lda. This policy
says what it does with data. Last changed on 12 November 2026.

## In short

The app has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and it sends your position nowhere.
Apple's map, which the app starts with, loads the area on screen from Apple. A map download shows
our server your IP address, and the server keeps no record of it. If you write to us, we receive
what you send.

## Location and motion

- The app uses your location only while it is on screen, to show how fast you are moving and where
  you are on the map. It never asks for your location in the background.
- It keeps one value: the top speed of the day and the time you reached it, in the app's own
  settings on your iPhone. That value is part of your iPhone's backup, and the app sends it nowhere.
- Motion data tells the app when your iPhone is lying still. It is not stored.
- You can turn off the app's access to location at any time in the iPhone's Settings, under Privacy
  & Security.

## Maps

- **Apple Maps** is the map the app starts with. Apple loads the area on screen from its servers, so
  Apple sees that area and your IP address. Apple's privacy policy applies to that. If you choose
  Outlines or OpenStreetMap in Settings, the map loads nothing from the internet.
- **Outlines:** these are built into the app and need no internet.
- **OpenStreetMap:** a country is downloaded only when you tap Download in Settings, from our server
  maps.1201.de. Like any download, the request shows the server your IP address, which country's map
  you asked for, your language setting and the versions of the app and of iOS. It does not include
  your position. The server keeps no record of downloads: no access log and nothing per person, only
  the total bytes it sent each day and month. It writes no line about the app's downloads. Only a
  malformed request, which the app never sends, or a fault inside the web server itself can leave an
  error line with the address it came from, and the system log keeps such a line until it rotates,
  which can take months. The server is rented from a hosting provider, which only carries the
  traffic. We use your address only to deliver the file you asked for and to keep the server
  running, which is our legitimate interest under Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR.

## When you write to us

If you use the form at https://1201.de/#contact, we receive your name, your email address and your
message by email, together with the IP address the form was sent from, the country derived from it
and your browser's identification, which names the browser, its version and the operating system.
Cloudflare runs the form and delivers the message for us. We use it only to answer you, as you
asked (Article 6(1)(b) of the GDPR), and keep it only as long as your question needs it. Issues on
GitHub are public, and GitHub's privacy statement applies to them.

## Links

Links in the app, such as this policy, the code on GitHub and the OpenStreetMap credits, open in
your browser, outside the app. The policies of those sites apply there.

## Your rights

The app itself stores nothing about you outside your iPhone, and deleting the app removes
everything it kept. For anything we hold, such as a message you sent us, you can ask through the
form at https://1201.de/#contact to see it, correct it, restrict it or delete it. You can object at
any time to our use of your address based on legitimate interest. You can also complain to a data
protection authority, which in Portugal is the CNPD (https://www.cnpd.pt).
